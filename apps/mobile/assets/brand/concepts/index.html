Kora — five logo concepts

Each shown at 256 / 64 / 20 px on the light (#F2F2F7) and dark (#0A0D0B) app backgrounds. Corners rounded only to preview the iOS mask; source SVGs are full-bleed squares.

A K emerges from a 3×3 grid of memory dots — the same constellation of foods lighting up again, recognition rather than decoration.

At 20 px the unlit dots all but vanish so it degrades to a blocky K, and dot grids carry some app-launcher baggage.

A messy organic blob (the photo of food) sits beside the three crisp macro bars it becomes — the app's entire job in one frame, with the three brand accents as the three macros.

It is a two-part diagram rather than a single ownable mark, and the three accent colours make it the busiest of the five.

A fork whose tines are drawn as a bar chart — food on first read, structured data on second; the tension the product actually resolves.

It flirts with food-delivery cliché, and if the bar-chart double-read fails to land it is just a fork.

Three log rows resolving over time — dots, then a dot with a partial bar, then one bright full bar: logging getting faster and surer as Kora learns you.

At sidebar size it can read as a generic list or menu glyph; the story depends on the brightness ramp surviving compression.

A noisy line — a photo, a spoken sentence — settles into a perfectly straight one and ends in a single logged dot: the messy real world resolving into a precise entry.

Horizontal-stroke marks are inherently quiet; at 20 px it stays elegant but has lower instant recognition than its bolder neighbours.
