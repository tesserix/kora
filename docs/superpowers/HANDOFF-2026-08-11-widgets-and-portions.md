**The headline fix.** Typing "one sachet of NESCAFE Mocha" used to resolve to
100 g / **545 kcal**. It now resolves to **1 portion / 90 kcal** — verified on
device before and after. This was the same defect `afe2db0` fixed for barcode
scans on 2026-08-11 morning; that fix covered only the barcode path, and text,
photo and voice had carried it ever since. `parsePortionGrams` matched a fixed
phrase table and defaulted to 100 g while `top.Item` — the resolved food, with
its own serving mass — sat unused at both call sites.

Precedence now: explicit mass → the food's own named serving → the curated
phrase table → a branded food's own serving mass → the flat default. Step four
is restricted to OpenFoodFacts provenance **deliberately**: USDA reference
servings are not portions anyone eats (`Turkey, whole, raw` is 5717 g), so
falling back to those would replace a mild underestimate with a 57× overestimate.
A test pins the turkey case at 100 g.

### Deleted, with evidence

`units.Fallback` guessed a serving mass from a word in the food's name. The
first production dry run planned 762 writes, of which only 123 came from a row's
own label; **395 of the other 639 matched a keyword that was not the food's head
noun**. `Apples, dried, stewed, WITHOUT added sugar` took sugar's 200 g cup.
Fried chicken "with flour" took flour's 125 g cup. `Alcoholic beverage, rice
(sake)` took rice's 158 g cup despite being a liquid.

Restricting it to head nouns was measured and rejected: 60 of the 244 survivors
(24%) were still wrong. The table is gone.

### A shipped bug this work uncovered and fixed

`useHealth` derived its status from `requestAuthorization`, which reports only
that the **prompt was presented** — HealthKit never discloses read
authorization. So the app recorded `"authorized"` even when denied, rendered a
confident **"Steps 0 of 10,000"** for unknown data, and gated the reconnect
affordance on a `"denied"` state that could never occur. Deny once and you were
stuck on a false zero with no way back. Fixed in `38ad2e9`, verified on device:
the card now reads "Connect Apple Health".

## The trap that caught me twice

**The simulator cannot exercise HealthKit reads at all.** Every read fails there.
That made "access denied" and "no data today" indistinguishable during local
verification, which is exactly why the first fix looked correct and would have
shipped a daily false prompt. Health logic validated only on the simulator is
validated against a permanently-failing read.

The correction came from a human saying "the last TestFlight build read steps
and sleep fine on a real device" — a single sentence that resolved a known gap
and exposed a bug in the same breath.

handoff held: **defects concentrated in the plan, not the implementation.**
